# Phase 1B simulation environment

`worlds/rbi_phase1.sdf` is generated from `../config/world_config.yaml` using SDF
1.9 and validated with installed SDFormat 14.9.0. The mission field is a finite
400 × 400 m slab, with top surface at z=0, centered at (0,0). World +X/+Y/+Z are
local east/north/up, respectively; distances are meters, angles radians, and no
real geographic origin is assigned.

Physics uses the installed default DART plugin, gravity `(0,0,-9.80665)` m/s²,
1 ms maximum step and requested real-time factor 1.0. There is no wind system;
wind velocity is zero. The baseline has no aircraft, controller or flight route.

Survey markers A/B are static visual-only models at (-175,0,0) and (175,0,0),
350 m apart. Their cap/post sizes are display styling, not position uncertainty
or flight-zone dimensions. The altitude reference is a visual scale at (0,100),
with its horizontal reference at 80 m AGL. A ground label and an upright label
identify it in the two views. Dimension lines, labels, grid, boundary, markers
and coordinate indicators contain **no collision elements**. Only the finite
0.2 m ground slab and the 3 × 3 × 0.08 m generic landing pad collide. The pad
center is at (5,-20) and its top is z=0.08 m; its location is a scene-layout choice.

The grid spans -200 to +200 on both axes at 50 m spacing: 9 lines each direction.
Gazebo's additional default 1 m GUI grid is explicitly disabled. The yellow
boundary is a visual field outline, not a physical wall or geofence. Grid lines
are raised slightly for rendering depth precision, with no contact geometry.

Labels are lightweight local stroke geometry using boxes, with no external
fonts, textures, meshes or network assets. The world has 20 static models,
150 visuals and 2 collisions. Shadows are disabled for this inspection baseline.
These choices keep the model small and make it usable offline.

## Reusable GUI views

From the project root, start `scripts/launch_world.sh server` and then either
`scripts/launch_world.sh gui` or `scripts/launch_world.sh topdown` in another
terminal. The saved configs are `gui/perspective.config` and `gui/topdown.config`.
Both initially include the whole field and use near/far clips of 5/3000 m and
horizontal FOV 60 degrees. Pose angles in the YAML config are radians; the
MinimalScene plugin's FOV field is explicitly converted to degrees.

The top-down camera is at z=700 m with +Y at the top of the image. The perspective
camera is at (340,-475,390) m. Near clipping at 5 m is chosen for this large-field
overview; close-up work may require lowering it. These camera poses are viewer
configuration, not vehicle commands or flight trajectories.

The installed Screenshot GUI plugin exposes `/gui/screenshot`. The runtime test
captures after its observation window because a capture requested immediately
at startup may be black before scene synchronization. See the actual
[perspective](../docs/screenshots/phase1b_perspective.png) and
[top-down](../docs/screenshots/phase1b_topdown.png) captures and their
[provenance](../docs/screenshots/provenance.json).

Use `python3 scripts/build_world.py` from the root to check config consistency,
and `--write` to regenerate deliberately. The older `tests/physics_probe.sdf`
remains an independent 100 m analytical sphere fixture and is not generated by
this Phase 1B world configuration. Its physical parameters are test values,
not assumptions about the user's aircraft.
